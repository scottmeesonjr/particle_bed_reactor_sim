# Q5: Can a fast trained model stand in for the solver?

Commit 19ce0e1. Procedure: docs/model.md A3 and A4. Ji et al. element, channel on, Z pattern.

## Answer
- **No, not by our rule.** Rule: held-out balanced accuracy >= 0.95 and peak-temperature MAE <= 50 K. Measured: balanced accuracy 0.915, MAE 41.5 K.
- **Speed.** 3 µs per prediction vs 0.53 s per solver case (about 166,865 times faster).
- **Outside the training range** (cold frit pore 4-6 µm, does not change the answer): accuracy 0.685, balanced accuracy 0.783, MAE 325.9 K on 42 converged cases.

## Data
- Budget: 2000 cases (0.53 s per case on 8 workers). Status counts: {"stable": 1748, "out_of_range": 243, "no_solution": 9}.
- Out-of-range set: 200 cases, status counts {"out_of_range": 152, "stable": 42, "no_solution": 6}.
- Train 1600 (1396 converged, used for the temperature model); held out 400.

## Held-out details
- Classifier: accuracy 0.978; confusion {"true_not_ok_pred_not_ok": 40, "true_not_ok_pred_ok": 8, "true_ok_pred_not_ok": 1, "true_ok_pred_ok": 351}.
- Temperature model: 352 converged cases, max error 258 K.

## Limits
- The surrogate only knows this element, channel on, Z pattern, and the sampled ranges.
- It learns the solver, not the reactor: anything the solver gets wrong, it inherits.
